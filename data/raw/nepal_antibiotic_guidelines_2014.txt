# National Antibiotic Treatment Guidelines (2014) - Nepal
## Ministry of Health and Population (MoHP)

### Overview
The National Antibiotic Treatment Guidelines (2014) were developed to address Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) and provide standardized guidance for antibiotic therapy in Nepal.

### Core Objectives
- Combat AMR: Reduce morbidity and mortality associated with antibiotic-resistant infections.
- Rational Use: Standardized guidance for selection, dosage, and duration.
- Preserve Effectiveness: Prolong the therapeutic lifespan of antimicrobial agents.

### Clinical Scope
Covers empirical and definitive treatment for:
- General Medicine
- Surgery
- Pediatrics
- Obstetrics and Gynecology
- Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)
- Ophthalmology
- ENT (Ear, Nose, Throat)
- Dental Infections

### Facility Levels
- Sub-health post / Health post
- Primary Health Care Center (PHCC)
- District Hospital

### Tiered Restrictiveness
- Non-restricted: General use.
- Restricted: Prescription by a Medical Officer or higher.
- Highly restricted: Reserved for specialists or consultants.

### Safety Guidance
Includes summaries for safety during pregnancy, lactation, and pediatric use. It also covers surgical prophylaxis and topical antibiotic use.

### Guiding Principles
1. Surveillance: Monitoring resistance trends.
2. Behavioral Change: Rational prescribing and reducing self-medication.
3. Infection Control: Strengthening hospital hygiene.

Note: These were succeeded by the National Antimicrobial Treatment Guidelines (2023) based on the WHO AWaRe classification.
